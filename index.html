Third-order products · 2A−B, 2B−A

Two-transmitter 3rd-order products land one channel-spacing outside each pair — the usual dropout culprit. Keep ≥250 kHz between any product and any carrier (Shure). Full coordination also checks 3-transmitter products (A+B−C) and receiver IM; this covers 2-transmitter only.

Time-Alignment Rack Console

d&b rig · Mains + Subs + Front Fills · delays in R1 · alignment only. Tap a step to tick it off; tap a heading to fold a section.

✓
TM1 → EVO4 in 1; reference → EVO4 in 2; +48V ON.
✓
Avantis Signal Gen → Pink Noise → to Mains/Subs/Fills and a spare matrix = reference back to in 2.
Good

You hear pink from the PA and the REF meter in Smaart moves.

Stuck?
  1. No PA sound → generator not routed to those buses, or master/matrix down.
  2. REF meter dead → the spare matrix isn't fed the pink, or the cable's in the wrong EVO4 input.
  3. REF present but subsystem silent → fine here; you solo them one at a time later.
Why

The reference must be the same pink noise, tapped before the R1 amp delays — any console output is, since delay lives downstream on the amps.

✓
Smaart: Options → Audio I/O = EVO4, 48k. TF: Meas = MIC (in 1), Ref = REF (in 2).
Good

Live magnitude, phase and coherence traces appear when pink is playing.

Stuck?
  1. Flat / no traces → EVO4 not selected as the device, or inputs not assigned to the TF.
  2. Traces but frozen → measurement not running; start the TF engine.
✓
Gains: both meters −18 to −12 dBFS, green, no clip.
Good

Both meters sit in the green, steady, never touching the top.

Stuck?
  1. REF way hotter/colder than MIC → adjust REF gain; a line reference sits well below mic gain.
  2. Clipping (red) → back the input down; a clipped input poisons coherence.
Why

Healthy, matched levels are the foundation of good coherence — get this right and half your "bad data" problems vanish.

✓
R1 delay unit = ms Extras → Options → Project.
✓
Mic on a stand, ear height, on-axis. Never handheld.
Phantom gotchaThe EVO4's 48V feeds both inputs. Fine for the mic. If the reference distorts / thumps → use a DI or a no-phantom path.
✓
Solo one Main. Find → Insert.
Good

Delay Finder returns a steady number, and after Insert the phase trace becomes a readable line, not a wrapped scribble.

Stuck? — Find won't lock / jumps
  1. Make sure only that one system is playing — mute everything else.
  2. Raise the pink level; too quiet = it can't find the peak.
  3. Raise averaging for a steadier read.
  4. Long throw (delay tower)? Use a bigger FFT — the FFT time constant needs to be ≥ 3× the delay you're measuring.
Why

Find locates the direct-sound arrival; Insert aligns Smaart's clock to it so phase means something.

✓
Coherence high & solid across your band?
Good

Coherence rides near the top and holds through the range you'll align — especially the crossover band. The data is trustworthy.

Stuck? — bad coherence
  1. Bad only down low? Often just too little level there — raise sub/pink level in that band.
  2. Raise overall pink level (safely) so signal beats the room.
  3. Raise averaging — more averages tame reflections in a live room.
  4. Get the mic closer / more on-axis to the box under test.
  5. Off hard surfaces — walls, tables, the deck — on a stand at ear height.
  6. Brutal reverberant room? Accept low-end dips and trust only the coherent crossover band. Don't chase perfect everywhere.
Why

Coherence = signal vs. everything else. You raise signal, average out reflections, grab more direct sound — or honestly accept a bad room's limits.

✓
Mic where both main & sub have energy.
Good

Main and sub magnitudes overlap somewhere around 80–100 Hz — that overlap is where you align.

Stuck?
  1. No overlap / sub barely shows → move out into the room, off the sub's null; don't measure jammed under or right behind the box.
✓
Solo Main: Find → Insert, then capture the trace (target = MAIN).
✓
Leave delay set. Solo Sub, Find. Add delay in R1 to the earlier one (shorter Find #).
Good

After you type the delay, the sub's live trace slides toward the captured MAIN.

Stuck?
  1. Unsure who's early? Find the main, note its ms; Find the sub, note its ms. Shorter = earlier = gets the delay.
  2. Trace didn't move → you edited the wrong amp channel, or R1 unit isn't ms.
✓
Fine-tune on phase: in the crossover overlap, nudge R1 delay until the sub's phase sits on top of MAIN's.
Good

Through ~80–100 Hz the two phase traces lie right on each other.

Stuck? — delay vs polarity
  1. Traces same shape, sliding as you add delay, and you can get overlap → it's timing. Keep nudging delay.
  2. Roughly parallel but ~180° apart, and delay only slides the overlap point without ever sitting together across the band → it's polarity. Flip sub polarity in R1, then re-fine the delay.
  3. Loop in order: delay first → one polarity-flip test → delay again. Don't alternate randomly.
Why

Delay shifts timing continuously; polarity is a fixed half-cycle flip. If the gap is ~half a cycle across the band, no delay closes it — only the flip does.

✓
Unmute both → want a summation bump, not a dip.
Good

Combined level rises smoothly through the crossover.

Stuck? — won't sum, dip stays
  1. Confirm the dip is at the crossover (~80–100 Hz), not elsewhere. Elsewhere = not your sub/main timing.
  2. Flip sub polarity in R1 and re-measure the sum. Dip fills in? Polarity was it — done.
  3. Still dipping → re-check delay: solo each, Find, verify you delayed the early one by the right amount; nudge a few tenths of a ms watching the sum.
  4. Levels within ~10 dB in the crossover? If the sub's way under the main, they can't sum — match levels first.
  5. Still? Move the mic a few feet. A dip that's a peak two feet away is a position null, not your alignment.
Why

A crossover dip = the two sources out of step in time or polarity, or a measurement-position null. Fix time first, polarity second, level/position third.

✓
Mic at a front seat the fill covers, on-axis.
✓
Solo Main: Find → Insert.
✓
Solo Fill (leave delay set): Find. The delta = delay to add to the fill.
Good

Find reports a positive delta = how much earlier the fill is. That's your number.

Stuck?
  1. Delta tiny / negative → mic may be too far from this fill, or the main isn't really reaching this seat. Reposition to where the fill actually covers.
✓
Type that into the Fill channel delay in R1.
✓
Re-Find the fill → delta should be ≈ 0 = aligned.
Good

Second Find reads ≈ 0 — fill and main now land together.

Stuck?
  1. Not near 0 → confirm you entered the delay on the fill (not the main), and that only the fill is soloed.
  2. Multiple fills → repeat at the seat each one serves.
✓
Unmute all. Check middle / side / front row.
✓
Self-test: muting one side of a pair should make it drop. Gets louder? Realign.
Why

Two aligned sources sum, so removing one lowers the level. If removing one raises it, they were cancelling — that pair's delay or polarity is off.
