  2. Traces but frozen → measurement not running; start the TF engine.

Both meters sit in the green, steady, never touching the top.

  2. Raise the pink level; too quiet = it can't find the peak.

✓
Coherence high & solid across your band?
Good

Coherence rides near the top and holds through the range you'll align — especially the crossover band. The data is trustworthy.

Stuck? — bad coherence
  1. Bad only down low? Often just too little level there — raise sub/pink level in that band.
  2. Raise overall pink level (safely) so signal beats the room.
  3. Raise averaging — more averages tame reflections in a live room.
  4. Get the mic closer / more on-axis to the box under test.
  5. Off hard surfaces — walls, tables, the deck — on a stand at ear height.
  6. Brutal reverberant room? Accept low-end dips and trust only the coherent crossover band. Don't chase perfect everywhere.
Why

Coherence = signal vs. everything else. You raise signal, average out reflections, grab more direct sound — or honestly accept a bad room's limits.

✓
Mic where both main & sub have energy.
Good

Main and sub magnitudes overlap somewhere around 80–100 Hz — that overlap is where you align.

Stuck?
  1. No overlap / sub barely shows → move out into the room, off the sub's null; don't measure jammed under or right behind the box.
✓
Solo Main: Find → Insert, then capture the trace (target = MAIN).
✓

Good

After you type the delay, the sub's live trace slides toward the captured MAIN.

Stuck?
  1. Unsure who's early? Find the main, note its ms; Find the sub, note its ms. Shorter = earlier = gets the delay.
  2. Trace didn't move → you edited the wrong amp channel, or R1 unit isn't ms.
✓
Fine-tune on phase: in the crossover overlap, nudge R1 delay until the sub's phase sits on top of MAIN's.
Good

Through ~80–100 Hz the two phase traces lie right on each other.

Stuck? — delay vs polarity
  1. Traces same shape, sliding as you add delay, and you can get overlap → it's timing. Keep nudging delay.
  2. Roughly parallel but ~180° apart, and delay only slides the overlap point without ever sitting together across the band → it's polarity. Flip sub polarity in R1, then re-fine the delay.
  3. Loop in order: delay first → one polarity-flip test → delay again. Don't alternate randomly.
Why

Delay shifts timing continuously; polarity is a fixed half-cycle flip. If the gap is ~half a cycle across the band, no delay closes it — only the flip does.

✓
Unmute both → want a summation bump, not a dip.
Good

Combined level rises smoothly through the crossover.

Stuck? — won't sum, dip stays
  1. Confirm the dip is at the crossover (~80–100 Hz), not elsewhere. Elsewhere = not your sub/main timing.
  2. Flip sub polarity in R1 and re-measure the sum. Dip fills in? Polarity was it — done.
  3. Still dipping → re-check delay: solo each, Find, verify you delayed the early one by the right amount; nudge a few tenths of a ms watching the sum.
  4. Levels within ~10 dB in the crossover? If the sub's way under the main, they can't sum — match levels first.
  5. Still? Move the mic a few feet. A dip that's a peak two feet away is a position null, not your alignment.
Why

A crossover dip = the two sources out of step in time or polarity, or a measurement-position null. Fix time first, polarity second, level/position third.

Field Rescue — No Audio & Hum/Buzz

Your chain: source → GX4816 → Avantis (SLink/gigaACE) → SQ5 · Avantis → Lake LM44 → AES → D40/D80 → box. Halve the path to the dead spot, then clean the noise. Tap a step to tick it; tap a heading to fold a section.

✓
Channel basics: fader up, not muted, gain/trim up, and assigned to the bus you're monitoring.
✓
A solo / PFL latched anywhere? It mutes everything else. Clear all solos.
Good

No solo/PFL lit on any channel or master; the mix returns.

Stuck?
  1. Check both desks — a latched PFL on the SQ5 (monitors) can be just as silent-making as one on the Avantis.
  2. Look for a lit AFL/PFL master or a soloed matrix.
✓
Source actually alive? Instrument/playback making sound. Condenser or active DI → +48V on. Wireless / active DI → battery.
Good

Meter on that channel twitches when the source plays.

Stuck?
  1. Dynamic mic on a dead channel? Phantom won't hurt it — but a flat battery in a wireless or active DI will kill it silently.
  2. Passive source, condenser expected → no +48V = no signal.
✓
Right patch? Source is in the input you think it is — check the sheet, not your memory.
✓
Output side up: main master, plus the matrix/output feeding that PA, up and unmuted.
✓
Amps live: powered, out of standby/mute, and the speakON actually seated & latched at the box.
Good

D40/D80 shows input and isn't muted in R1 or on the front panel.

Stuck?
  1. An NL4 that clicks in but isn't turned to latch can drop a box.
  2. Amp in standby or a channel muted in R1 looks powered but passes nothing.
✓
Go to the midpoint — the Avantis main/matrix meter, or the LM44 input. Signal there?
Good — you get a clean split

Signal present at the midpoint → the dead half is downstream (do step 3). Nothing → the dead half is upstream (do step 2). One look just halved the problem.

Why

Every check you make in the middle of the chain throws away half the suspects. Two or three halvings and you're standing on the fault — far faster than walking the whole path end to end.

✓
Upstream half (dead at the midpoint): meter at the Avantis input / preamp. Moving → it's console routing/mute/assign (back to §1). Dead → source, cable, or the GX4816 → SLink/gigaACE link.
Stuck? — chase it up
  1. Swap the cable first — cheapest failure, most common.
  2. Try the source in a known-good input. Works there? The original channel/preamp is the fault.
  3. Whole stagebox dark? Check the GX4816 → Avantis SLink link and clock — if the box lost sync you lose every channel on it at once.
  4. Confirm +48V if it's a condenser or active DI.
Why

A whole-stagebox dropout is a link/clock problem, not a channel problem — don't waste time on one input when 48 died together.

✓
Downstream half (signal leaves the console, box silent): check the LM44. In but not out → LM44 mute/routing or wrong drive line selected. Out OK but box dead → AES cable, wrong amp channel, amp standby/mute, or speakON unseated.
Stuck? — split the amp too
  1. LM44 is your drive-line switch point — make sure the right source (your Avantis, not a touring desk's lines) is selected and unmuted.
  2. Swap the box to a known amp channel. Alive there → the amp channel or its patch is the fault.
  3. AES carries two channels per XLR (D1/2, D3/4) — a half-seated AES cable can drop a pair.
  4. Box still dead on a good channel → it's the NL4 or the box.
✓
Name it. Low, smooth hum ≈ 60 Hz (+120/180) → power / ground loop. Harsher, buzzy, harmonic-rich → dimmers (SCR lighting), digital hash, or RF.
Why

Buzz is just hum with more and higher harmonics. Light dimmers chop the AC waveform on and off mid-cycle, throwing high-frequency harmonics that both radiate through the air and ride the mains wiring into your audio — which is why buzz sounds sharper than a plain power hum.

✓
Find the culprit. Pull all inputs — noise gone? Reconnect one at a time until it comes back. That device (or its ground) is your source.
Good

Silence with everything unplugged, and the hum returns on exactly one reconnection — you've found it without guessing.

Stuck? — still humming with nothing plugged in
  1. Then it's internal / power, not a source loop — suspect the amp/processor's own AC feed and grounding.
  2. Same bisection, different layer: split the power — move audio to a different circuit and listen.
✓
Fix #1 — common power. Get all audio gear on one source / same circuit & ground. Don't daisy-chain strips; check the earths.
Why

A ground loop needs two paths to earth at different potentials. Put all the audio on one clean feed and the loop often just disappears — the cheapest fix there is, and the first to try.

✓
Fix #2 — ground-lift the offending line, one end only. Flip the DI ground-lift, or lift the cable shield at one end. Never both ends.
Stuck?
  1. Lift at the receiving end by preference; leave the source shielded.
  2. Lift both ends and the shield floats — you trade hum for RF pickup and lose the safety of the screen. One end, always.
Why

Lifting one end breaks the loop while the shield still drains interference from the other end. This is an audio ground — see the warning below about mains.

✓
Fix #3 — isolation transformer. Still there? Drop an iso / transformer-DI into that line — it breaks the audio ground path and keeps the signal.
Never do thisNever lift or “cheat” the mains / AC safety ground (the third pin, or a ground-lift adapter on the power) to kill hum — it makes the gear dangerous and unsafe to touch. Lift audio grounds only, one end. If a hum only dies when you defeat the safety earth, you have a real wiring fault — fix the power, don't float it.
✓
Pin-1 problem? One box hums or grabs RF no matter the source, often worse when you wiggle the connector shell → shield current is leaking into that box's signal ground.
Stuck? — you can't rewire it on site
  1. It's a build fault inside the gear (pin 1 routed through signal ground instead of straight to chassis) — not something you fix at the gig.
  2. Field moves: use a different input or box, drop an iso-transformer in front of it, keep it off long cable runs.
  3. Flag it for the shop so it gets corrected properly.
Why

The cable shield should land straight on the chassis. When a maker routes it through the internal audio ground, shield noise and RF ride into the signal — and no cable swap of yours will cure a fault that lives inside the box.

✓
Buzz that tracks the lights? Dimmer/SCR buzz. Get audio power off the lighting phase, keep audio mains clear of dimmer feeds — and expect some is radiated, so reposition cables and gear too, not just re-patch.
Phantom gotcha48V is safe on modern dynamics and phantom-ready ribbons over balanced XLR — but a vintage ribbon (centre-tapped, grounded transformer) or an unbalanced / jack connection can be damaged. Phantom OFF before you patch an unknown ribbon.

Gain Structure — Preamp to Box

Your chain: preamp → Avantis → matrix → Lake LM44 → AES → D40/D80 → box. Set it once, keep unity through, don't crank downstream to fix a weak start. The numbers below are for your gear. Tap a step to tick it; tap a heading to fold.

Your targets

On the Avantis, 0 on the meter = +4 dBu = −18 dBFS, peak LED at −3 dBFS — about 18 dB of headroom to clip. LM44 takes up to +26 dBu in / +21 dBu out. D40/D80 accept up to +25 dBu (AES 0 dBFS = +27.3 dBu) at a fixed 31 dB gain. Set the level once at the top; hold it here at every stage.

✓
Pad hot sources at the DI first (bass, keys, active pickups) — don't starve the preamp to tame a hot source.
✓
Avantis preamp: bring gain up so the channel meter averages ~0 dB (= +4 dBu = −18 dBFS), with peaks below −3 dBFS (the peak LED).
Good

Meter breathing around 0, the peak LED only flickering on the loudest hits — never sitting lit.

Stuck?
  1. Peak LED / clip lit → back the gain off, or pad at the source. A clipped input can't be fixed downstream.
  2. Sitting way below 0 → too cold; you'll fight hiss all night. Bring the gain up now, at the source.
Why

Connector Pinouts

Pin by pin for the connectors you actually terminate — signal, data and network. Power (cam-lock) is on its own card.

XLR — 3-pin analog audio

Pin 1
Shield / ground — cable screen, chassis.
Pin 2
Hot — signal +, in phase.
Pin 3
Cold — signal −, return.
Pin 2 = hot is the modern standard (AES14 / IEC 60268-12); numbers are molded on the shell. Some vintage gear is pin-3-hot — check before you trust a polarity.

DMX512 — 5-pin (and 3-pin) XLR

Pin 1
Signal common (ground).
Pin 2
Data 1 − (primary link).
Pin 3
Data 1 + (primary link).
Pin 4
Data 2 − — 5-pin only, rarely used.
Pin 5
Data 2 + — 5-pin only, rarely used.
5-pin is the ANSI E1.11 standard; 3-pin (pins 1–3 only) is common but non-compliant. Don’t run DMX on mic cable — wrong impedance, flaky on long runs.

speakON — NL4 / NL8 speaker

1+ / 1−
Amp channel A — the standard single-channel pair.
2+ / 2−
Channel B, or the HF leg when bi-amping.
NL8: +3± / 4±
Two more channels down one cable (tri-/quad-amp).
A 2-conductor cable lands on 1+/1−. An NL4 plugs into an NL8 chassis and just uses 1–2. Your d&b V/Y boxes terminate on NLT4 (in + link).

etherCON / RJ45 — Ethernet & Dante (T568B)

1 · 2
White/Orange · Orange — pair 2.
3 · 6
White/Green · Green — pair 3.
4 · 5
Blue · White/Blue — pair 1.
7 · 8
White/Brown · Brown — pair 4.
Both ends T568B (the common one) — just match them. Gigabit uses all four pairs; Dante needs Gigabit past ~32 channels. etherCON is a locking shell over a normal RJ45 — same pinout.
